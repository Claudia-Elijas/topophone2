Topophone2#

topophone2 is a Python package for probabilistic wavefield decomposition using receiver arrays. Given the array covariance matrix (cross-spectrum) derived from recordings made by a set of receivers, it estimates the mixture of incoming wavefields that produced the recorded signal: inluding plane waves, directionally distributed arrivals, propagating environental noise and sensor self-noise.

For every component, it estimates the full posterior probability distribution (not just a single best-fit value) of the parameters that define the signal’s energy distribution, such as the direction of arrival, slowness and directional spread. It also quantifies how well the data constrain each component, and compare competing models through Bayesian evidence.

Topophone1 The original topophone was an acoustic instrument patented in in 1880 by Alfred M. Mayer, with the purpose of finding the direction of a sound source, such as a ship’s fog horn. It used a pair of ear trumpets set apart from each other: the listener turned the device until the sound in both ears was balanced.

The problem#

Arrays of receivers (e.g., seismometers, hydrophones or microbarometers) often record several wavefields at once in the real world. Coherent signals from distinct sources overlap and energy is spread over a range of directions. Many array-processing methods assume (a) a far-field point source and (b) incoherent noise. In practice, however, (a) the energy is often distributed over a range of directions, and (b) ambient environmental noise propagates coherently across the array. Distributed wavefields cause a loss of coherence between sensors, which can bias the results of conventional methods. Nevertheless, a parametrisation of the energy distribution over slowness can be obtained from the inter-sensor covariance, given an analytical model of this coherence loss, such as a plane wave or a normal or angular-harmonic distribution. This is a nonlinear, non-unique and often ill-conditioned inverse problem, therefor linearised asymptotic uncertainty estimates are often biased. These estimates are important for loction and monitoring decisions. Therefore, a nonlinear probabilistic approach is needed to properly quantify the uncertainty in the inferred parameters.

topophone2 therefore provides a probabilistic framework for decomposing the array covariance into a mixture of parameterised signal components and for quantifying the uncertainty in the inferred parameters.

The approach#

topophone2 treats decomposition as a Bayesian inverse problem, in three steps:

1. Forward model

ArrayCovarianceModel predicts the array covariance for a given receiver geometry and frequency band, as a superposition of parameterised, incoherently distributed energy sources. Each source type is described analytically by its characteristic function.

2. Likelihood

The predicted and observed array-covariance matrices are compared, using an exact complex Wishart likelihood. Uniform priors bound every parameter.

3. Variational inference

Normalising flows (built on GeoPVI) learn an approximation to the posterior. Samples drawn from it can be summarised using marginal statistics such as means, credible intervals and information gain. Bayes factor can be used to compare different models.

The whole workflow runs from a single call to topophone2.topophone.decompose().

Functionality#

Applications

topophone2 works with any array of receivers whose array-covariance matrix can be estimated. Typical uses include:

  • direction-of-arrival estimation, with uncertainties;

  • estimating signal-to-noise ratios;

  • characterising directional spreading of energy, such as scattered or multipath arrivals;

  • quantifying the directionality of ambient noise fields;

  • comparing competing source models through Bayesian evidence.

Features

  • Analytical array-covariance forward models for point sources (plane waves), normally and skew-normally distributed energy in slowness, isotropic noise (2D or 3D), anisotropic noise fields expanded in cylindrical or spherical harmonics, and user-defined characteristic functions.

  • Exact gradients of the forward model, for efficient gradient-based optimisation.

  • Probabilistic inversion by normalising-flow variational inference, with optional mean-field approximation.

  • Optional joint inversion with a noise-only covariance to constrain environmental noise and sensor self-noise.

  • Posterior analysis tools: marginal densities, credible intervals, information content and evidence (via harmonic).

  • Utilities for signal processing (FFT, windowing, Slepian tapers, sample covariance estimation, beamforming) and for reading and writing input data and configuration files.

Quick start#

After installing the package, a decomposition looks like this:

from topophone2.utils.read import read_txt
from topophone2.topophone import decompose

# Observed array covariance, receiver positions and frequencies
freq, x, Gamma_obs, N_obs = read_txt("data_input.txt")

decompose(
    Gamma_obs    = Gamma_obs,
    N_obs        = N_obs,
    x            = x,
    freq         = freq,
    sigs         = ["point", "normal"],   # signal components to fit
    prior_bounds = (lower, upper),        # uniform prior bounds per parameter
    N_flow       = 10,
    learning_rate = 1e-2,
    N_iterations = 2000,
    N_montecarlo = 10,
    N_samples    = 5000,
    outpath      = "results",
)
